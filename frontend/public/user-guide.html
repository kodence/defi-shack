00

Before you start

What the data is, and isn't

LPSim reads live Uniswap V3 data from The Graph for Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base. Everything on Discovery and every live simulation is real on-chain history — not estimates.

Two things are worth knowing up front, because they change how you read the screens:

Preset mode
Opening /simulator with no pool selected loads one of five demo presets built on synthetic price history. It is there to explore the mechanics. Never size a real position from it — always arrive at the simulator through Simulate on a Discovery row.
Fee APR only
Every APR in LPSim is fee income. It excludes divergence loss by design, which is why steps 05 and 06 exist. An APR you like is only half an answer.
The loop, in one line

Screen on Discovery → simulate one pool properly → save it to the Portfolio → stress the whole book → deploy on the DEX → watch it on Track. LPSim never touches your wallet or signs anything; it is a research and monitoring tool, and you execute elsewhere.

01

Find candidate pools

Discovery · FATE — fundamentals, APR, TVL, exposure

The table loads the top pools by TVL for the networks you select. Sensible floors are already applied (TVL ≥ $1M, APR ≥ 1%, fees ≥ $1K, volume ≥ $1M), so you are starting from liquid pools, not the long tail.

Fast path

  1. Pick your networks and a Timeframe — 30 days is the sane default.
  2. Type the assets you actually want to hold into Filter by asset (e.g. ETH, WBTC, LINK).
  3. Hit FATE filters to apply the framework's bounds in one click.
  4. Sort by Fee/TVL and open the best two or three with Simulate.

Reading the columns

Four columns do the real screening work. The rest is context.

Fee/TVL
Daily fees divided by TVL — how hard each dollar of liquidity is working. The single best "is this pool actually busy" signal. It turns green at ≥ 0.059%, the FATE threshold for an actively traded pool.
Vol CV
Volume consistency: the variation in daily volume. Lower is steadier. Turns red above 100%, which means volume is erratic and any APR built on its average is fragile.
Correlation
Pearson correlation of the two tokens' USD prices, with 7d and 30d underneath so you can see the trend. Above 0.5 is the FATE floor. Rising 7d correlation after a divergence is a good entry signal.
Price Volatility
Max deviation from the mean over the timeframe. Under 15% keeps divergence loss predictable. This is usually the column that eliminates a pool.
Correlation reads 0.00% on stablecoin pairs — that's correct, not broken

A stablecoin's USD price is flat, so correlation against it is mathematically undefined and reported as zero. That is also why FATE filters deliberately omits the correlation bound: applying it would wipe out every ETH/USDC-style pool. Judge correlation yourself, and only for crypto-to-crypto pairs.

If FATE filters returns nothing, the market is calm — not the app

The preset demands APR 30–500%, TVL ≥ $1M and volatility < 15% simultaneously. In a quiet stretch, blue-chip pools sit near 15–18% volatility and few clear the APR floor. Widen the volatility bound with the small filter icon in that column header, or add networks — Base and Arbitrum usually carry the higher-APR pools.

Position sizing starts here

Note the pool's TVL before you leave. FATE caps a position at 5% of pool TVL — up to 10% when the same pair has deep liquidity in another pool. LPSim checks this for you later, but choosing a pool that is too small for your intended size wastes the whole analysis.

02

Frame the pool

Simulator · pre-simulation setup

Arriving via Simulate loads real candles, real volume and the pool's real liquidity distribution. Three things to settle before you touch the range.

Orientation
LPSim auto-orients so the price reads naturally — a stablecoin becomes the quote, so you see 2,475 USDC/WETH, not 0.0004 WETH/USDC. ⇅ flip swaps it. The flip is instant and local: it inverts the chart and your bounds without refetching, and APR is unchanged. Match whatever your DEX shows so the range you set here is the range you paste there.
Current price
Taken from the pool's own tick. Override it only to model a "what if price were here" scenario — for real setup, leave it alone.
Investment (USD)
Set your real deployment size now. LPSim dilutes APR by your own liquidity, so a large deposit into a thin pool visibly lowers the projected return — which is exactly the effect you want to see before committing.
Set the holding period before anything else

Holding period (days) cascades through the entire page: it sets the calculation window to 2× your holding period, defines the move size the range guard tests against, and sets the recovery horizon that grades every divergence scenario. Change it first and every downstream verdict is aimed at your actual plan.

03

Pick the range

Simulator · RANGE — time, correlation, fluctuation, trend, exposure

A tighter range earns more per dollar but exits sooner. The framework resolves that trade-off with your holding period, not with a guess.

Fast path

  1. Set Holding period (days) — the Calc window follows at 2×.
  2. Click a width pill (±5% … ±25%) as a starting point, or drag the Min and Max lines directly on the price chart.
  3. Read the Range guard and widen the side it flags.
  4. Slide the exposure control until the token split matches your market view.

The range guard

This is the framework's "notable fluctuations" test, computed from the pool's real candles. It compares your bounds against the largest moves that actually happened in the window — both intraday spikes and full holding-period trends — and warns when a repeat would push you out:

⚠ Down: −11.3% seen · range −10.0%

Translation: the pool fell further than your lower bound within a single holding period in the last 90 days. Widen the downside, shorten your holding period, or accept that you will be rebalancing. A guard that passes both directions (✓) means your range has survived recent history.

Exposure targeting

The slider under the token split shifts the range to hit a target ratio while preserving its width — so you change your directional bias without changing how tight the position is. Match it to your view:

ViewSplitWhat the position does
Neutral — sideways market50 / 50Pure fee harvesting; the pool sells rallies and buys dips around you.
Moderately bullish60/40 – 65/35More of the base asset, so you keep more upside.
Strongly bullish70/30 – 80/20Heavy base exposure. Needs real conviction — you are closer to holding than to LPing.
Accumulatingstable-heavyThe pool buys the asset as it falls; track your effective average entry.

Watch the 30d In-Range Prob. card as you work. It is a volatility model, not a promise, but a range showing 20% is telling you something the APR number won't.

04

Get an APR you trust

Simulator · realistic vs worst-case

The headline APR depends entirely on how much liquidity you assume you are competing with. LPSim reconstructs the pool's real tick-level liquidity, so you can ask that question four ways.

Calculation methodAssumesUse it for
Current priceLiquidity at the current tickThe realistic default.
Peak in rangeThe most crowded point in your rangeWorst case. Use it when a liquidity wall sits next to the current price — price tends to hover there.
Avg. in rangeLiquidity averaged across your rangeA smoothed middle estimate.
Custom priceLiquidity wherever you pointModelling a specific level you expect price to sit at.

Cross-check the Liquidity distribution chart underneath the candles. Green bars are inside your range; a tall wall next to the current price is the "institutional yield-stealing" pattern the VALID framework warns about, and it is the reason Peak in range can halve your APR.

Volume basis

7d / 21d / 30d sets how much history feeds the fee estimate; trim spikes (on by default) drops days above 3× the median so one anomalous session cannot inflate the projection. The panel tells you exactly what it used, e.g. $72.0M/day over 30d · 1 spike day excluded.

The number that matters is the pair

The APR card shows your realistic figure with the worst-case beside it. Deploy only when both still look attractive. A pool that reads 43% at current price and 17% at peak distribution is really a 17% pool with upside — size it accordingly.

05

Stress divergence loss

Simulator · the go / no-go test

Divergence loss is what you give up versus simply holding the two tokens. The question is never "is there any?" — there always is — but how many days of fees it takes to earn it back.

The panel pre-loads scenarios in both directions, including the largest moves these two tokens actually made together in their history (tagged real). Add your own by typing each token's percentage move and pressing Simulate.

VerdictRecoveryMeaning
fast≤ holding periodFees repay the loss within your planned horizon. Green light.
ok≤ 2× holding periodAcceptable, especially for short horizons. Watch it.
slowbeyond thatThe position cannot earn its way out at current fees.

Read it differently by pool type

Crypto – crypto
Recovery speed is the whole test. Simulate both directions; you want fast or ok on each. A pair that only survives one direction will eventually meet the other.
Crypto – stable
Divergence loss is a feature, not a bug. The pool sells the asset as it rises and buys as it falls. Expect slow verdicts and judge the position on income and the exposure you wanted instead. LPSim says so in the panel footer.
Stable – stable
Ignore divergence loss; only total fee income and depeg risk matter.

The Scenario analysis table beside it runs the same logic across a grid of price moves, with its own Recover column — a quick way to see where the position stops making sense.

07

Size the portfolio

Portfolio · allocation and stress testing

Individually sound positions can still make a fragile book. This page asks whether they hold together.

Risk grades and the backbone

Every saved position is scored out of 100 on volume consistency, recovery, correlation, depth and pool size, then graded A ≥ 75, B ≥ 55, C below that. The best risk-adjusted position is tagged ★ Backbone — that is your largest allocation, and everything else sizes down from it.

Allocations

Edit Invested (USD) inline. Each edit re-runs that position, so APR moves as your size dilutes the pool — watch a large allocation eat its own return. The summary strip keeps weighted APR and projected daily and monthly fees current.

The stress test

Set a market-wide move and run it. Stablecoins stay pinned at $1 while every other asset moves together, and LPSim aggregates divergence loss, post-move value and how many days of portfolio fees repay it.

What a healthy book looks like

Positions offsetting each other. In a real −20% run, a correlated WBTC/WETH position took zero divergence loss while an ETH/USDC position took a real one — that is diversification doing its job. Aim for a recovery time inside a couple of weeks of fees; if the whole book needs months, it is too correlated or too tightly ranged.

Where this lives

The portfolio is stored in your browser, not on a server. It is private to that browser and profile, and clearing site data clears it.

08

Track and rebalance

Track · SMART — situation, method, analysis, range, timing

Paste any wallet address and LPSim pulls its live Uniswap V3 positions across the selected networks. No connection or signature — it is public chain data, so you can watch any address.

The four numbers

Value
What is in the position now, and the token split it has drifted to.
Unclaimed fees
Computed from on-chain fee growth — exact, not estimated.
Net vs HODL
The line that matters for crypto–crypto. Fees minus divergence loss, against four benchmarks: your initial assets (the minimum bar), a 50/50 split, and all-of-either token. 3–4 green is strong, 2 is worth monitoring, 0–1 means the pool or the range needs work.
Earnings retention
How much of your fee income you actually keep after divergence loss. Think of APR × retention as your realistic APR. The trend matters more than the level.

Those four numbers describe the position right now. Underneath each card, LPSim also shows how they have moved over time — see in-range history.

When to rebalance

LPSim raises SMART flags on each card. The framework's timing rules are the valuable part:

  • Wait 48–72 hours before acting on an out-of-range position unless it is clearly deteriorating — price often comes back.
  • The best moment is your original entry price, where divergence loss is roughly zero. LPSim flags it when price returns within ±2%.
  • Prefer adding capital over swapping. Adding fresh capital to restore your ratio avoids realising the loss; swapping locks it in and may trigger tax.
  • Execute on consolidation, in quieter volume, with gas in mind — not in the middle of the move.
  • Exceptions that justify acting immediately: a clear trend change, major fundamental news, or a fast move on heavy volume.

Simulate this pool on any card reopens the simulator for that pool so you can design the replacement range before you touch anything.

Fees shown are unclaimed only

The Uniswap subgraph reports corrupted lifetime collected-fee data (the second token mirrors the first), so LPSim excludes it rather than showing a wrong number. For a position that has already claimed fees, your true earnings and true Net vs HODL are better than displayed. Passive positions that never claimed are exact.

09

In-range history

Track · what the position did while you weren't watching

A position outside its range earns nothing. The In range badge tells you about this instant; in-range history tells you what share of the time the position has actually been working — which is the number that decides whether the range you picked in step 03 was right.

How history accrues

LPSim records a snapshot of every position: its range status, price, value, unclaimed fees and divergence loss. There are two sources, and you only control the second.

Loading the page
Every wallet lookup writes a snapshot, debounced to once a minute so refreshing doesn't flood the record. Free, but it only captures the moments you happened to be looking.
☆ Watch
The backend then snapshots that wallet every 15 minutes on its own, so history keeps building while the page is closed. This is the one that produces a meaningful percentage. It is opt-in because each poll spends metered Graph queries — watch the wallet you actually manage, not every address you look up.
★ Watching
Click again to stop polling. Everything already recorded is kept — unwatching pauses collection, it does not erase history.

Reading the panel

In-range %
Share of observed time spent in range, weighted by duration rather than by snapshot count. Colour-coded ≥ 90% 60–90% < 60%.
Timeline
The same history drawn proportionally to time: in range, out of range, and unobserved. Long red stretches show you when the position stopped earning, not just how often.
Coverage
How much of the elapsed window was actually observed. Read this before you trust the percentage above it.
A high percentage at low coverage means "not enough data yet"

Gaps longer than 90 minutes — the backend was down, or the wallet wasn't watched — are recorded as unobserved rather than credited to your last known state. That is deliberate: downtime lowers coverage instead of quietly inflating in-range time. So 100% in range at 3% coverage is not a healthy position, it is an unmeasured one. Wait for coverage to build before acting on it.

Earnings retention trend

The same snapshots drive the retention sparkline — net vs HODL divided by earnings, tracked over time, with its direction marked ▲ flat ▼ and its starting value for comparison.

Retention is the share of your fee income you actually keep after divergence loss, so APR × retention is the return you are really earning. The direction matters more than the level: rising retention means fees are pulling ahead of divergence, and falling retention means the opposite, even while the position sits comfortably in range.

Acting on it

What you seeWhat it meansDo this
High in-range and rising retention The range fits the pair's real volatility and fees are outrunning divergence. Leave it alone. Reuse this width next time.
Low in-range, position still open The range is too tight or off-centre for how this pair actually moves. Return to step 03, widen or recentre, and check the range guard before redeploying.
In range, but retention falling The two assets are diverging faster than fees accumulate. Recheck correlation (step 01) and recovery time (step 05). A crypto–stable pool is expected to look like this.
Flat 0% in range Price left and has not come back; you are earning nothing. Apply the SMART timing rules in step 08 — wait 48–72h first unless it is clearly deteriorating.

Over several positions this becomes the most honest feedback loop LPSim offers: your projected APR was a model, but in-range time is what actually happened. If your ranges consistently spend a third of their life out of range, the fix is in how you set them — not in the pool you picked.

Tuning and retention of the data

LPSIM_POLL_INTERVAL_MS changes the snapshot cadence — shorter gives denser history at the cost of more queries. Snapshots older than 180 days are pruned automatically. Everything lives in a local SQLite file (backend/data/lpsim.db), so history is per-machine and is not shared between installs.

10

Positions elsewhere

Track · custom positions

For pools LPSim cannot see — another DEX, another chain — track them by hand. Same analytics, entered manually.

  1. ＋ Add custom position: token symbols plus their CoinGecko API ids (ethereum, lido-dao) for live pricing, the fee tier, and your price range. Exchange and network are labels for your own filtering. Check the base/quote orientation matches your DEX. For anything CoinGecko doesn't list, set a manual USD price instead.
  2. Deposit: exact token amounts, total USD value, gas, and the timestamp — from your DEX transaction or a block explorer.
  3. Log check: paste four live values from the DEX position page — both token balances, unclaimed fees per token, and the current pool price. Takes about 30 seconds and refreshes every metric on the card.
  4. Claim and Withdraw record fee claims and the exit. Withdrawing closes the position, preserving its history and final P/L.

Custom positions compute value, earnings, divergence loss, net vs HODL, overall P/L including gas, and in-range status. Fee APR appears once there is at least a day of history. Like the portfolio, they live in your browser.

Work faster

Shortcuts that save the most time

  • Set the holding period first. One field aims the calc window, range guard and every divergence verdict at your actual plan.
  • Drag, don't type. The Min, Max and Now lines on the price chart are draggable and re-simulate as you release.
  • Width pill first, exposure slider second. The pills set how tight the range is; the slider then shifts it without changing that width.
  • Compare two methods, not four. Current price and Peak in range bracket your realistic and worst cases; the others are refinements.
  • Never retype a pool. Use Simulate from a Discovery row and Simulate this pool from a tracked position — both carry the pool through.
  • Flip is free. Orientation changes are local; no reload, no refetch, APR unchanged.
  • Reset before a new hunt. Reset filters restores the defaults; the per-column filter icons handle one-off bounds without disturbing the rest.
  • Watch selectively. Every watched wallet costs metered queries on each poll. Watch the wallet you actually manage.
  • Read coverage before the in-range number. A percentage built on 3% coverage is noise; the same number at 90% coverage is a verdict on your range.
  • Compare fee tiers of the same pair. Discovery lists them separately — the 0.05% and 0.3% pools for one pair often have very different Fee/TVL.

What it doesn't cover

Known limits — read before sizing anything

Uniswap V3 only
Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base. Other venues go in as custom positions.
Fee APR excludes IL
Every APR is fee income. Divergence loss is modelled separately and never netted off automatically.
Collected fees excluded
Upstream subgraph data is corrupted, so tracked earnings are unclaimed fees only — conservative for positions that already claimed.
Correlation on stables
Reports 0 against a stablecoin by construction. Meaningful only for crypto–crypto pairs.
History needs uptime
In-range time only accrues while the backend runs. Coverage is reported so you can see how much was actually observed.
Local storage
Portfolio and custom positions live in one browser profile. No sync, no backup — clearing site data clears them.
Models, not guarantees
In-range probability, stress moves and projected APR are models built on past behaviour. Markets are free to do something new.
LPSim is research, not advice

It never connects a wallet, holds funds, or executes anything. Every deployment, claim and rebalance happens on the DEX, by you, with your own judgement.

Running LPSim

Local setup

npm run dev
Starts both servers — API on :3001, app on :3000. Run it from the repository root.
THE_GRAPH_API_KEY
Required, in the root .env. Everything on Discovery and Track depends on it.
LPSIM_POLL_INTERVAL_MS
Snapshot cadence for watched wallets. Default 15 minutes; lower it for denser history at the cost of more queries.
LPSIM_DB_PATH
Moves the history database. Default backend/data/lpsim.db.
npm run verify:history
In backend/ — checks the in-range time-attribution math against synthetic series.
First run is slow

Discovery fetches and computes metrics for hundreds of pools per network, then caches for five minutes. The first load after a restart takes a while; every load after it is immediate.
